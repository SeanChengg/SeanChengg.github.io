This project involves reverse engineering the robotic wrist joint of the LIMS2-AMBIDEX research robotic arm, developed by Prof. Yong-Jae Kim at KOREATECH.
My interest in animatronics and compliant robotic systems began with early sculptural and design work. To better understand the mechanics behind dynamic movement, I analyzed several humanoid robotic arms and chose the LIMS2-AMBIDEX for reverse engineering. This project allowed me to study cable-driven actuation, lightweight joint design, and mechanical control systems that manage precise motion, focusing on solutions for human-safe robotic arm movement.

Configuration of Actuating Wires
LIMS2 is a robotic arm that achieves human-safe, high-speed motion by minimizing joint inertia while maintaining high structural stiffness. The actuators and reduction gears are placed at the proximal end of the arm, and motion is transmitted to the joints using a cable-driven system combined with block-and-tackle pulleys. This configuration amplifies output force while keeping the distal sections lightweight, closely matching the mass and dynamic properties of a human arm.

LIMS2

This project extends the LIMS Elbow with a wrist prototype featuring a spherical rolling joint ("quaternion wrist") that mimics natural motion through quaternion-based cable tendon control. By eliminating singularities, the design achieves organic, fluid movement. The 1:1 replica combines 3D-printed PLA parts, machined components, and Arduino Uno-driven continuous servos, controlled via a dual-axis joystick.

Physical Prototype

03

Due to limitations in simulating the joint's full motion range in CAD, this functional prototype validates kinematics and prevents mechanical conflicts. Programmed with Arduino, it demonstrates precise pitch/yaw/roll control, ensuring reliability for high-speed human-robot interactions.

This diagram illustrates the design of three-DOF spherical rolling joint.

 

Three parallel links connect the base to the distal frame, allowing smooth rolling motion.

 

A pair of tension wires is pre-wound around the blue pulley four times to amplify the pulling force.

 

The green-marked shaft, equipped with two universal joints, transmits rotational motion from the bottom servo to the distal end.

Prototype Diagram
04

To control pitch, Servo 2 pulls the wires while Servo 3 feeds them, both running at the same speed according to the joystick input. Pushing the joystick left moves the joint one way; pushing it right moves it the opposite way.

For yaw, a modulo routine drives Servo 1 in a four-step cycle:

  1. first click → counter-clockwise rotation,
  2. second click → stop,
  3. third click → clockwise rotation,
  4. fourth click → stop again.
